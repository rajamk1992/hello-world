## 1. What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in a diversified portfolio of assets (stocks, bonds, cash, etc.) managed by a professional fund manager. Investors receive units proportional to their investment.

## 2. Key Terms

* **AUM (Assets Under Management):** Total market value of assets managed by the fund. Larger AUM often implies scale but not necessarily better performance.
* **AMC (Asset Management Company):** The firm that runs the mutual fund (e.g., fund house).
* **NAV (Net Asset Value):** Price per unit of the fund. Calculated as: `NAV = (Market value of assets - liabilities) / total units outstanding`.

## 3. Types of Mutual Funds

**By asset class:** Equity, Debt, Hybrid, Liquid/Ultra-short.

**By style/strategy:** Large-cap, Mid-cap, Small-cap, Multi-cap, Sectoral, ELSS (tax-saving), Index funds, Fund of Funds.

## 4. Equity vs Debt (Comparison)

* **Equity funds:** Invest primarily in stocks. Higher return potential, higher volatility. Suitable for long-term goals (5+ years).
* **Debt funds:** Invest in bonds, government securities, corporate debt. Lower volatility and lower expected returns. Useful for short- to medium-term goals or capital preservation.

## 5. Costs & Charges

* **Expense Ratio:** Annual fee charged by the fund (expressed as a % of AUM). Covers fund management, administration, distribution (for regular plans).

  * *Example:* If expense ratio = 1.2% and AUM = ₹100 crore, annual expenses ≈ ₹1.2 crore.
* **Exit Load:** A charge when investors redeem units within a specified period. Example: 1% exit load if redeemed within 1 year.
* **Taxes:** Capital gains tax depends on holding period and asset class (check your country’s tax rules).

## 6. Dividend Options & IDCW

* **Growth option:** Earnings are reinvested in the scheme; NAV reflects growth.
* **Dividend / IDCW option:** Fund pays out income to investors. When IDCW is declared, NAV drops by approx the payout amount.

### IDCW (Income Distribution Cum Capital Withdrawal)

IDCW is the newer term many AMCs use instead of "dividend". It represents distributions made to investors from the fund’s earnings or capital.

### IDCW — Detailed Example

* Suppose you hold 1,000 units. NAV before IDCW = ₹20. Fund declares IDCW ₹2 per unit.
* Payout = 1,000 units * ₹2 = ₹2,000 (paid to investor)
* NAV after payout ≈ ₹18 (ignoring taxes and other adjustments)
* Total value immediately after = 1,000 * 18 + 2,000 = ₹20,000 (same as before, ignoring tax and transaction timing)

## 7. How Returns are Calculated

* **Absolute return (%)** = `(Ending NAV - Starting NAV + Distributions) / Starting NAV * 100`
* **CAGR** = `((Ending value / Starting value)^(1/years)) - 1`

### Total return with dividends

Include IDCW or reinvestment value when calculating total return.

## 8. Risk Measures (brief)

* **Volatility (standard deviation):** Measures how much returns vary.
* **Beta:** Fund’s sensitivity to market movements.
* **Sharpe Ratio:** Excess return per unit of risk.

## 9. How to Evaluate a Fund

* Look at long-term performance (3-5 years+), not just 1 year.
* Compare fund’s performance against its benchmark and peer group.
* Check consistency: how often has it outperformed the benchmark?
* Consider expense ratio and portfolio turnover.
* Check fund manager tenure and strategy stability.

## 10. Practical Tips for Investors

* Use **SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)** for rupee-cost averaging.
* Diversify across asset classes and fund styles.
* Keep an emergency fund in liquid/ultra-short funds.
* Avoid frequent switching; focus on goals and time horizon.
* For short-term needs (<1 year), avoid equity funds.

## 11. Quick-formula Cheat Sheet

* NAV = (Assets - Liabilities) / Units
* Absolute return (%) = `(End NAV - Start NAV + Distributions) / Start NAV * 100`
* CAGR = `((End / Start)^(1/n)) - 1`

## 12. Glossary

* **SIP:** Systematic Investment Plan
* **Lumpsum:** One-time investment
* **AUM:** Assets Under Management
* **AMC:** Asset Management Company
* **NAV:** Net Asset Value
* **Expense ratio:** Annual fund charge
* **IDCW:** Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal

### Appendix: Example — Expense Ratio Impact (Simple)

If two funds return 12% gross but have expense ratios 0.5% and 1.5%:

* Net return (Fund A) = 12% - 0.5% = 11.5%
* Net return (Fund B) = 12% - 1.5% = 10.5%
  Over long horizons this difference compounds significantly.
